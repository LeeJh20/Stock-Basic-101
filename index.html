Stock Basics: Supply & Demand

A stock is a share of ownership in a company. Prices move based on how many people want to buy versus sell the stock — this is the basic idea behind supply and demand.

What is a stock?

Buying a stock means owning a small part of a company. If the company grows and performs well, the stock may become more valuable over time.

How markets work

Stocks are bought and sold on exchanges where investors trade shares based on price, news, expectations, and company performance.

Supply & Demand

When more people want to buy a stock than sell it, demand rises and price often goes up. When more people want to sell than buy, price often falls.

Simple rule to remember

Price changes are often driven by investor behavior. Hearing a company has strong potential can increase demand, while fear or bad news can create selling pressure.

Scam Alert!

Not every “hot stock tip” is a real opportunity. Some investment scams promise fast money with little risk, but they often rely on pressure, confusion, or false promises.

1. Pump-and-Dump Schemes

Someone promotes a stock widely to inflate its price, then sells their shares before the hype fades.

2. Guaranteed Return Claims

If a person promises quick profits with no risk, that's a major red flag. Real investing carries risk.

3. Fake Investment Groups

Social media groups may pressure people to buy a “secret” stock or pay upfront fees for “exclusive access.”

Red flags to watch out for

  • Guaranteed high returns with no explanation
  • Urgent messages pushing immediate action
  • Fake reviews or viral social media hype
  • Unlicensed sellers or unverifiable claims
